**The week in three sentences:** The leadership crisis at Nvidia turned into a broad sell-off in AI and chip stocks that took the S&P 500 down 8.6% for the week and more than 10% below its October record, with Asia's chip-heavy markets hit even harder. Strong Nvidia results and a record-breaking Anthropic IPO could not turn the mood, while Fed minutes and hawkish speakers made a December rate hike look almost certain. Gold, copper and silver fell with stocks as investors sold what they could to raise cash, and over the weekend Seoul and Beijing announced measures to steady their markets.

## The big themes of the week

### 1. The AI sell-off becomes a correction

The week began with Nvidia under pressure after Jensen Huang's resignation as chief executive the week before. On Monday Nvidia fell 4.1% and the S&P 500 1.9%, a third straight loss, and AP reported that the chipmaker had lost $735 billion in market value since the scandal began. ([AP via WSB-TV](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/business/stocks-decline-asia/Y6KSUFUT6E4JJJKTTJKBZ6637U/))

Nvidia's results on Tuesday evening, the first under interim CEO Colette Kress, were strong: revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% on the year, and a fourth-quarter outlook of about $130 billion that assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/)) The shares rose at Wednesday's open and then closed lower. By Friday the selling had spread to the whole sector: Nvidia fell about 14%, AMD 13.3%, Micron 13.9% and Broadcom 12.2%, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index dropped 10.7% in one day. The S&P 500 lost 3.7% on Friday, its seventh straight decline, and closed more than 10% below its 6 October record, the usual definition of a [correction](/learn/correction-and-bear-market/). The Nasdaq Composite is now down for the year. ([AP via Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/business/asia-shares-drop-ai/XAHACU4WBU4XZABWUTHRLPBXPE/))

Legal pressure grew at the same time. California's attorney general served Nvidia with a subpoena on 19 November and said on Friday that the probe into statements about chip sales to China covers possible criminal violations of state law; he stressed it is preliminary. The SEC is also investigating, according to reports the SEC has not confirmed. Over the weekend President Trump wrote that he hopes the board rehires Huang; the board has said it does not intend to. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/california-widens-nvidia-probe-to-criminal-law-a-bigger-deal-than-huang-return-talk/)) Market fear rose sharply: the [VIX](/learn/vix/) closed at 35.89 on Friday, up 86% on the week (Yahoo Finance).

What to watch: Nvidia's answer to the subpoena, any detail on the SEC inquiry, and whether chip stocks can stabilise after the relief bounce in US futures on Monday morning.

### 2. Anthropic lists in the largest IPO on record

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Wednesday, above the planned $70 to $78 range, and raised about $81 billion, the largest IPO on record according to AP. The stock opened at $111.50 on Thursday, closed its first day at $97.17 and fell 8.5% to $88.91 on Friday, still above the issue price. AP notes the company is not profitable under standard accounting and has committed to about $900 billion of computing and infrastructure spending in the coming years. ([AP via ADN](https://www.adn.com/business-economy/2026/11/19/anthropic-begins-trading-as-a-15-trillion-company-in-the-biggest-ipo-on-record/), [AP via Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/business/asia-shares-drop-ai/XAHACU4WBU4XZABWUTHRLPBXPE/)) More on [IPOs](/learn/stocks-and-ipos/).

### 3. A December Fed hike is almost fully priced

Minutes of the Fed's October meeting, released on Wednesday, showed nearly all officials expect a quarter-point increase in December and two raised the possibility of half a point. ([AP via ADN](https://www.adn.com/business-economy/2026/11/18/fed-minutes-higher-interest-rates-needed-in-december-as-inflation-worries-mount/)) On Friday Governor Lisa Cook warned the Fed risks falling "behind the curve", and CME FedWatch put the chance of a hike on 8 to 9 December at 97.5%. ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-november-19-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-anthropic-nvidia-141644188.html))

Bonds still rallied as investors fled stocks: the 10-year Treasury yield fell 24 [basis points](/learn/basis-point/) over the week to 4.91%. Over the weekend the debate shifted. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the Fed may be making a policy mistake if it overtightens, while Governor Waller has argued for half a point and Goldman Sachs economists expect hikes in December and March. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-us-futures-hold-2-bounce)) Tighter policy is spreading: the Swiss National Bank's vice chairman said on Sunday the bank is watching for second-round effects after Swiss inflation jumped to 1.5%, which markets read as pointing to a December hike. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/central-banks/snb-s-martin-eyes-second-round-effects-after-inflation-jumps-to-1-5-hike-in-view/))

### 4. Gold and metals sold for cash

[Gold](/learn/gold/) futures lost 9.5% over the week to $3,515.40, their lowest level in about 14 months and more than a third below January's record above $5,500. ING pointed to higher rate expectations and a firmer dollar, and Commerzbank to forced selling as investors raised cash to cover equity losses; silver lost about 14%. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/commodities/gold-sinks-9-in-a-week-as-margin-calls-and-fed-hike-bets-override-its-haven-role/)) [Copper](/learn/copper/) futures fell 9.9%, with a 5.3% drop on Wednesday that reports linked to the dollar, doubts about AI data-centre demand and weak Chinese data. ([Mysteel](https://www.mysteel.net/analysis/5149504-daily-copper-falls-as-fed-rate-hike-bets-mount-and-ai-demand-hopes-fade)) [Oil](/learn/oil/) was calmer: Brent ended the week 3.4% lower at $92.57 but is still up about 52% this year because of the Iran war.

### 5. Asia's chip markets take the hardest hit

Markets with a large weight in AI hardware suffered most. On Friday the Nikkei 225 fell 5.1%, Korea's Kospi 5.2% and Taiwan's Taiex 5.7%, which took the Taiex down 14.9% for the week. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)) The emerging-markets ETF lost 11.6% for the week, more than developed markets. Trade policy added uncertainty: the US closed a loophole that let Chinese firms rent AI computing abroad and added licence requirements for several Southeast Asian countries and Brazil ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/11/16/nation/trump-administration-moves-close-ai-chip-loophole-before-china-trip/)), and Xi Jinping and Donald Trump met at the APEC summit in Shenzhen without reported agreements on chips. ([China's Foreign Ministry](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng./xw/zyxw/202611/t20261119_12063139.html)) Over the weekend Korean officials began preparing a market stabilisation fund of about 10.8 trillion won (about $8 billion), and China's exchanges cut the margin requirement for buying stocks on credit to 80% from 100% from 30 November. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-us-futures-hold-2-bounce))

## Longer-term trends

- **Paying for AI.** Investors are questioning how much debt is being used to finance data centres. Anthropic's commitments of about $900 billion and Nvidia's $130 billion quarterly outlook show the scale of spending; the MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index is about 18% below its June peak on these worries. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-us-futures-hold-2-bounce))
- **A global tightening cycle.** The Fed, possibly the SNB, and the Bank of Korea (meeting Thursday, another hike expected) are raising rates while growth slows in Japan and Australia. This is unusual during an equity correction and is the main reason bonds and gold have not behaved as classic havens.
- **Energy after the Iran war.** Brent is up about 52% this year. Goldman Sachs expects it to fall to $78 by the end of 2027 as the market moves into surplus, and sees European gas prices roughly halving next year. These are forecasts, not facts. ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/investinglive-asia-pacific-market-news-us-futures-hold-2-bounce))

## Die großen Themen der Woche

### 1. Der KI-Ausverkauf wird zur Korrektur

Die Woche begann mit Druck auf Nvidia nach dem Rücktritt von Jensen Huang als Vorstandschef in der Vorwoche. Am Montag fiel Nvidia um 4,1 % und der S&P 500 um 1,9 %, der dritte Verlusttag in Folge; laut AP hatte der Chipkonzern seit Beginn des Skandals 735 Mrd. $ an Börsenwert verloren. ([AP über WSB-TV](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/business/stocks-decline-asia/Y6KSUFUT6E4JJJKTTJKBZ6637U/))

Nvidias Zahlen am Dienstagabend, die ersten unter Interimschefin Colette Kress, waren stark: 115,8 Mrd. $ Umsatz, ein Plus von 103 % zum Vorjahr, und ein Ausblick für das vierte Quartal von rund 130 Mrd. $, der keine Rechenzentrumsumsätze mit China unterstellt. ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/)) Die Aktie stieg zur Eröffnung am Mittwoch und schloss dann im Minus. Bis Freitag erfasste der Verkaufsdruck die ganze Branche: Nvidia fiel um rund 14 %, AMD um 13,3 %, Micron um 13,9 % und Broadcom um 12,2 %, der PHLX-Halbleiterindex verlor an einem Tag 10,7 %. Der S&P 500 gab am Freitag 3,7 % nach, der siebte Rückgang in Folge, und schloss mehr als 10 % unter seinem Rekord vom 6. Oktober, der üblichen Definition einer [Korrektur](/learn/correction-and-bear-market/). Der Nasdaq Composite liegt nun im Jahresminus. ([AP über Boston 25](https://www.boston25news.com/news/business/asia-shares-drop-ai/XAHACU4WBU4XZABWUTHRLPBXPE/))

Zugleich wuchs der rechtliche Druck. Kaliforniens Generalstaatsanwalt stellte Nvidia am 19. ### 3. Eine Zinserhöhung der Fed im Dezember ist fast vollständig eingepreist
